Design architecture

Modern facilities are shifting from static, hardware-centric buildings toward flexible, software-defined, energy-aware infrastructure. Our designs follow that shift.

Power, cooling, network, compute, storage, security and observability are designed as one operating system for the facility.

Our approach

Integration is the point.

The strongest designs combine resilient physical systems, automated operations, efficient cooling, dense compute support, and security controls that span both digital and physical layers.

  • Hybrid and distributed architectures
  • High-density compute for AI and accelerated workloads
  • Liquid cooling and advanced thermal management
  • Energy efficiency and sustainability
  • Maximised water utilisation efficiency
  • Modular and scalable facility design
  • Resilience beyond traditional redundancy
Design pillars

What each layer has to deliver.

01 — Efficiency

Measured properly

Power usage effectiveness still matters, but a modern sustainability program also accounts for water consumption, carbon intensity, equipment lifecycle, renewable sourcing, heat reuse, and embodied carbon in construction materials.

02 — Waste

Designed out

Modular electrical systems, variable-speed cooling, improved airflow containment, battery energy storage, smarter workload scheduling and detailed metering — reducing waste without giving up resilience.

03 — Resilience

Beyond redundancy

N+1 and 2N remain important. Resilience now also means software failover, multi-site replication, cyber recovery, supply chain planning, operational drills and dependency mapping.

Edge sites

Designed for when nobody is on site.

Edge facilities place compute closer to users, sensors, industrial systems, retail sites, telecom networks and autonomous systems. Their designs prioritise compactness, remote management, ruggedisation and fast replacement.

  • Remote observability across facility and IT layers
  • Standardised spares held to a common bill of materials
  • Tamper detection on enclosures and access points
  • Automated provisioning for rapid replacement
  • Out-of-band management independent of the primary path
Recommended design principles

Six rules we hold ourselves to.

Mixed densities
Design for a range of rack profiles rather than assuming one uniform density across the hall.
Shared telemetry
Make power and cooling telemetry visible to both facilities and IT operations teams — one view, not two.
Repeatable modules
Standardise modules for speed, but keep enough flexibility to absorb new accelerator and cooling requirements.
Core requirements
Treat automation, observability and security as core design requirements — not optional management layers added later.
Measurable sustainability
Set targets for energy, water, carbon and hardware lifecycle that can actually be reported against.
Tested resilience
Exercise resilience across people, processes, software, networks and facility systems — not just the switchgear.

Our facilities are aligned to the business and workload requirements of our customers.
