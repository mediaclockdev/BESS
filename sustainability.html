Home — Sustainability

AI shouldn't drink Australia dry

High-capacity AI data centres need enormous amounts of electricity and water. In an already water-challenged country, that is not a detail to leave until commissioning.

Water usage effectiveness

0.06 litres per kilowatt-hour. Two shot glasses.

WUE measures litres of water consumed per kilowatt-hour of IT load. Lower is better. Our target is 30 times more efficient than the industry average of 1.8 L/kWh.

Industry average 1.80L/kWh
PRDC target 0.06L/kWh

Estimates suggest that by 2030, AI data centres worldwide could consume enough water each year to match the drinking needs of roughly 1.3 billion people.

30×More efficient than average
Closed loop

The same water, over and over.

PRDC deploys closed-loop cooling systems that continuously recycle the same water instead of drawing a constant fresh supply. The design allows operation with almost zero ongoing water consumption, saving millions of litres per data centre every year.

  • PRDC data centres are designed to use recycled water
  • Collaboration with water utilities to connect to existing recycled networks
  • Supply meeting the stringent requirements of Class A recycled water
  • Fresh water draw per unit of compute far below industry norms
Cooling modes

Melbourne's climate does most of the work.

Majority of the year

Free-air cooling

Melbourne is cool and dry for most of the year. Our free-air system draws outside air directly into the data halls, so the facility runs without a meaningful water draw.

Hottest summer days

Chiller-based cooling

Only when outside air is too hot or humid do we switch to chillers, which use — and reuse — water to cool the air before it enters the halls.

Beyond PUE

What a modern sustainability program measures.

01

Water

Consumption per unit of compute, source of supply, and the share drawn from recycled networks.

02

Carbon

Operational carbon intensity alongside the embodied carbon in construction materials.

03

Energy

Power usage effectiveness, renewable sourcing, heat reuse and workload scheduling.

04

Lifecycle

Equipment lifespan, augmentation over replacement, and recyclability at end of life.

05

Metering

Detailed metering that makes power and cooling telemetry visible to facilities and IT alike.

06

Targets

Measurable goals for energy, water, carbon and hardware lifecycle — reported, not asserted.
